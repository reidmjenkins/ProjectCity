# The Last Tenant of Unit 9 — Chapter Outline

*A novella in 12 chapters plus an epilogue. Target ~28,000 words (about 100 paperback pages), ~2,300 words per chapter.*

Season structure: the book covers **one year**, which loosely mirrors the game's seasons. Each chapter introduces at least one game system so the book doubles as a design reference.

---

### Part One — Arrival

**1. Everyone Has a Place** *(spring)* — Wren arrives by relocation train. Officer Teague assigns her Unit 9 and the courier job, fits her band, hands over her Wake allotment. She meets Odile next door, who smells of something Wren can't place. First Evening Word. First curfew.
*Systems: arrival, band/Standing, Wake allotment, curfew, the Evening Word.*

**2. The Route** — First day at the depot. Marcus, the cork board, the bag. Jory shows her the shortcuts and the cameras' blind spots "just so you know where they are." First clean patrol stop with Officer Mirren. Wren learns the rhythm of the day and the weight of a clean record.
*Systems: courier shifts, route knowledge, patrol stops, camera density.*

**3. The Smell of Coffee** — Odile invites her in: real coffee, brewed in secret from a hoard. Wren tastes something that's *hers* for the first time. Jory takes her to Pell's Repairs in the Old Market. He offers her a package and a bag with a false bottom. Her first smuggling run; the risk shown plainly, the fear real.
*Systems: contraband, fences, concealment levels, the risk scale.*

**4. The Sergeant** — A routine stop goes wrong: Sergeant Grane, not Mirren. Jory tries to bribe and fails; Wren talks them both through it. Lost goods, a fine, a Standing ding. Afterward, Wren starts cultivating Mirren: small talk, a coin, his daughter's cough.
*Systems: patrol ranks, bribe vs. talk, fines, cultivated officers.*

### Part Two — Two Doors

**5. Gratitude Day** *(summer)* — The state festival. Rally in Founders' Square; Your Neighbor speaks "live." Marcus takes Wren under his wing: a clean courier can apply for a licensed farm permit. Benedetta at the permits desk explains the cost. Wren decides to try it the legal way.
*Systems: state festivals, legal track, licensed farm permits, Bureau of Choice.*

**6. Behind the Wall** — Wren saves for the permit. She also plants three coffee seeds from Pell behind a loose panel in Unit 9, because Odile's hoard is running out. Two doors at once. The coffee sells well, too well: the **Coffee Crackdown** begins. More stops, higher prices. Wren realizes the city *noticed*, but not her.
*Systems: hidden grow-rooms, passive income, Campaigns.*

**7. Lantern Stair** — Jory shows her the Undercity under a dead fountain. The Mole Market, Grandmother Tock, a tunnel creature that nearly takes Jory's hand. Wren finds a waterlogged Halloway newspaper that describes the Settlement completely differently.
*Systems: the Undercity, scavenging, conflict, artifacts.*

**8. The Lamplighters** *(autumn)* — An underground gathering in an old tram station. Nim recruits Wren; Candle's voice on the radio. Wren starts running banned books. A small, odd detail: Candle mentions something Wren only ever said to Teague.
*Systems: underground events, the resistance, banned media.*

### Part Three — What the City Knows

**9. Transparency** — A legal delivery takes Wren into Transparency House. She meets Ansel Rook and glimpses the Quiet Room. Ansel shows her her own file. It's thicker than it should be. Lamplighter leaflets on his desk, on Transparency paper stock.
*Systems: Ministry Quarter, Bureau of Transparency, clearance.*

**10. Unit 8** *(winter)* — Odile is taken in the night. Wren spends everything she has, savings for the permit included, buying Standing and favors from Mirren to get her back. Odile returns after nine days, smiling, taking her Wake, calling Wren "dear" like a stranger would. Who reported her? Wren suspects everyone, including herself.
*Systems: buying Standing, consequences, relationships, Bureau of Passion.*

**11. The Courts** — Wren goes deeper than ever, to Abel Quarry's Unredacted Library. She learns the Settlement was carried out by a group that called itself the Lamplighters. She finds one more document she wishes she hadn't: a duty roster with Candle's sign-off and a Bureau stamp. Or maybe a forgery. Quarry: "Every lamp needs someone to light it, and someone to own the oil."
*Systems: deep Undercity, the circular revolution hint.*

**12. Unit 9** — The last week. Three offers arrive at once: Teague offers a clearance transfer to the Heights; Candle offers "a larger part"; Grane, off-duty, offers a warning. Wren hides her notes in the flat, leaves her bag in Jory's locker, waters the coffee one last time, and walks out after curfew. Mirren sees her pass Checkpoint 3. He doesn't stop her.

**Epilogue — Arrivals** *(spring, one year later)* — A new relocation number steps off the train. Teague: "Unit 9. Courier. Everyone has a place." The new arrival asks who lived there before. "No one. It has always been vacant." Behind the bed, something green is growing.
