# Project City — Story Bible

*Companion to the novella* **The Last Tenant of Unit 9**. *Living document. Started 2026-11-22.*

The novella is the game's **backstory**. It follows **Wren Adair**, the courier who lived in the player's flat and worked the player's route one year before the game begins. Wren meets the whole cast, is pulled into both the legal and the dark track, and then disappears. The player arrives into Wren's empty flat. Everything Wren left behind becomes something the player can find.

**Tone:** cozy with creeping dread. Warm neighbors, small joys and dry satire on the surface. The menace grows slowly underneath and is never fully explained.

| Thing | Name | The irony / double meaning |
|---|---|---|
| The Party | **The Consensus** *(proposed)* | Sounds like "everyone agrees." Hidden inside it: *census*, being counted. Agreement is mandatory. |
| The city | **Freehold** *(proposed)* | A freehold is land you own outright. Nobody in Freehold owns anything (until the player buys property). |
| The old city underneath | **Halloway** *(proposed)* | The city the Consensus took over. Run by the **Halloway Council** before the "Settlement." |
| Face of the Party | **Your Neighbor** ✅ | "Your Neighbor is watching out for you." |
| Records | **Bureau of Transparency** ✅ | Redacts everything. |
| Provision | **Bureau of Choice** ✅ | You choose nothing. |
| Order / police | **Bureau of Liberty** ✅ | The police. |
| Wellness | **Bureau of Passion** *(proposed)* | It sedates everyone. |
| Assignment | **Bureau of Opportunity** ✅ | It tells you who to be. |
| State drug | **Wake** ✅ | A sedative. |
| The resistance | **The Lamplighters** *(proposed)* | "We keep the lights on." The Consensus may literally be keeping them on. |
| The takeover | **The Settlement** *(proposed)* | Official word for the day the Consensus took power. Sounds like peace, a dispute settled. |

Other in-world terms:
- **Standing**: everyone's Citizen Standing score, shown on the wrist-band (the **band**).
- **Unassigned**: the worst thing you can be. People say it in a whisper.
- **The Evening Word**: Your Neighbor's nightly broadcast at 8:00, right before curfew.
- **Grey**: street slang for black-market goods ("is it grey?").
- **Moles**: people who live and trade in the Undercity.
- **Scrip**: money. New arrivals get a small allowance for "personal sundries"; fines are taken out of it.
- **"Is it grey out?" / "Getting greyer."**: Pell's handoff passphrase.
- **Clear**: what a band shows after a clean patrol stop.

## 2. The Cast

Each character has a **game role** so we can build them as NPCs. The novella introduces them all.

### The protagonist
**Wren Adair** (she/her), 27. Relocation number **4471-K**. Arrives from an unnamed outer settlement with one bag. Practical, observant, quietly funny, slow to trust and quick to care. Wants something she can't name at first: a thing that's *hers*. By the end she has tried both tracks and seen too much.
- **Game role:** never seen in person. Her presence is everywhere: notes hidden in Unit 9, her name scratched out of the depot log, a coffee plant gone wild behind a wall panel, NPCs who mistake the player for her or refuse to talk about her. *Where she went is never answered.*

### Arrivals Block (starter zone)
**Odile Fenn** (she/her), 70s. Wren's next-door neighbor in Tenement 14. Sharp-eyed, warm, nosy. Skips her Wake. Keeps a single illegal basil plant in a teapot. Remembers Halloway. Teaches Wren that small, real things matter.
- **Game role:** the player's first neighbor and friendship NPC. After the novella she is "different" (see Ch. 10): kind but foggy, taking her Wake. Deep friendship slowly brings back flashes of the old Odile. Possible informant? *Never confirmed.*

**Marcus Bell** (he/him), 50s. Dispatcher at the **Arrivals Courier Depot**. Big laugh, clipboard, believes in the system the way some people believe in weather. Not cruel, just sure. Pushes Wren toward the legal track: clean record, licensed farm permits, a real future.
- **Game role:** courier boss, tutorial guide, legal-track mentor. He'll tell the player, cheerfully, that the route "has always been unassigned until now."

**Jory Kask** (he/him), 29. Fellow courier, fast talker, faster runner. Funny, a little reckless, generous. Carries grey goods inside his delivery bag. Introduces Wren to coffee, the Old Market and the Undercity. Romance thread with Wren, unresolved.
- **Game role:** dark-track mentor and a romance option. He's still grieving Wren and won't say so. Gives the player their first concealment bag "because someone should use it."

**Officer Dace Mirren** (he/him), 30s. Beat cop at the Arrivals checkpoint. Tired, decent, underpaid, has a sick kid. Takes small bribes and feels bad about it. Wren cultivates him over the novella.
- **Game role:** the first "cultivated officer." Low detection skill, bribeable. Knows something about the night Wren vanished.

### Bureau faces
**Assignment Officer Teague** (he/him), ageless. Bureau of Opportunity. Soft voice, perfect manners, never blinks enough. Assigned Wren, will assign the player. Uses the phrase *"Everyone has a place."*
- **Game role:** the Opportunity face. Job changes, clearance. Says Unit 9 had "no previous tenant."

**Benedetta Lusk** (she/her), 40s. Clerk at **Choice** (the ration store) and permits desk, Bureau of Choice. Brisk, bored, secretly romantic, loves the forbidden novels she confiscates.
- **Game role:** the Choice face. Rations, farm permits, property. A buyer for banned books.

**Sergeant Hollis Grane** (she/her), 40s. Bureau of Liberty. Precise, not cruel, very good at her job. She notices *patterns*. The person who almost catches Wren, and maybe the reason she stops.
- **Game role:** the higher-rank patrol with strong detection. Hard to bribe; must be *talked* to. A long-term rival who might, eventually, respect you.

**Dr. Seraphine Ode** (she/her), 50s. Bureau of Passion counselor at the **Wellness Kiosk**. Gentle, gives out Wake like candy, believes she is helping. Says "How are we *feeling*?"
- **Game role:** the Passion face. Malaise checks and Wake allotments. Treated Odile after Ch. 10.

**Ansel Rook** (he/him), 30s. Junior redactor at the Bureau of Transparency. Nervous, brilliant, ink-stained cuffs. Hates what he does and does it perfectly. Smuggles small truths out in the margins.
- **Game role:** the Transparency face. Ministry Quarter contact. Can "find" or "lose" records. Knows Wren's file is gone.

### The dark side
**Pell** (they/them), age unclear. Runs a kettle-and-radio repair stall in the **Old Market**. Fence for coffee and grey goods. Kind, careful, speaks in prices.
- **Game role:** first fence/vendor. Buys coffee, sells compartments and seeds.

**Nim** (she/her), 19. Lamplighter runner. Bright, angry, idealistic, very brave. Recruits Wren.
- **Game role:** resistance contact. Gives the player "lamp jobs" (banned media runs). True believer; doesn't know what the player might suspect.

**Candle** (unknown). The Lamplighters' leader. Only a voice on a radio band, or a typed note. Always knows a little too much.
- **Game role:** issues big resistance missions. *Never seen.* (The central hint: see Secrets.)

**Abel Quarry** (he/him), 80s. Hermit archivist of the **Unredacted Library** in the Undercity. Once a Halloway Council clerk. Speaks like a rare book.
- **Game role:** Undercity lore keeper. Trades history (documents, artifacts) for supplies. Has Wren's last letter, and won't give it to the player until trust is very high.

**Grandmother Tock** (she/her). Matriarch of the Moles' market under the Old Market. Trades old tech, knows the tunnels.
- **Game role:** Undercity vendor and guide to the layers.

### The voice
**Your Neighbor**: a warm, ordinary man on every screen. Cardigan, a mug, a kitchen-table set. Talks like your favorite uncle. Each Evening Word ends: *"Rest easy. Your Neighbor is watching out for you."* No one has ever seen him anywhere but on a screen.

## 4. Secrets (what's true, what's only hinted)

Rules for writers: **hint, never confirm.** Every hint should have an innocent explanation too.

1. **The Lamplighters are run by the Consensus.** Hints in the novella:
   - Lamplighter leaflets are printed on the same cream paper stock as Transparency forms (Ansel notices).
   - Candle once quotes a line from Wren's private relocation interview.
   - Teague says "Everyone has a place." Candle signs off "Everyone has a part."
   - Crackdowns always land *just after* the Lamplighters finish moving a shipment, never before.
2. **The Consensus began as Halloway's own resistance.** Abel Quarry's archive shows the Settlement was carried out by a group that called itself the Lamplighters. *The revolution is circular.*
3. **Odile was taken, and returned "Waked."** Who reported her? Candidates: Marcus (for her own good), Jory (to protect Wren), Odile herself (to protect Wren), or nobody at all: routine. *Never answered.*
4. **What happened to Wren?** Four possible readings, all supported: relocated (Teague), arrested (Grane), escaped into the Deep (Quarry), or recruited into the Consensus itself (Candle). *Never answered.* Players will argue about it, and that's the point.
   - *Seeded in Ch. 4:* Wren tells Sergeant Grane that her neighbor in Unit 8 won't take her Wake. Wren will later wonder if she reported Odile herself without meaning to.
6. **Wren wasn't the first.** Marcus and Jory insist Route Nine is "new" and was "unassigned." But a wall panel in Unit 9 has already been taken off once (Ch. 2), and Pell keeps an older, faded courier bag stitched **9** with a false bottom (Ch. 3). The circle runs back before Wren, too. *Never explained.*
7. **Your Neighbor might not be one person.** Small continuity slips in the broadcasts (a mug that changes hands). Only noticed by careful players.

## 5. How the novella feeds the game

| From the novella | In the game |
|---|---|
| Wren's notes hidden in Unit 9 | Collectible "Wren's Notes" (like Stardew's secret notes). Each one is a scene from the book in her words. |
| The coffee plant behind the wall | The player's first coffee seeds and the hidden grow-room tutorial. |
| Jory's bag | First concealment item. |
| Mirren's sick kid (Pia, 6, on children's Wake) | A friendship quest: bring honey-and-thyme remedy (grey) or report it (legal). |
| Pell's chalk slate, risk 1–5 | The visible risk scale UI. Pell is the in-world reason the player can read risk. |
| Grane reading route patterns from band data | Detection based on behavior: repeating the same blind-spot short cut raises suspicion. |
| The Coffee Crackdown (Ch. 6) | The first Campaign the player triggers. |
| Odile "Waked" | A long friendship arc with real payoff. |
| Abel Quarry's letter | A late-game reward on the dark/Undercity side. |
| Ansel's file | A late-game reward on the legal/Ministry side. |
| Both tracks reach the same truth | Legal and dark players each reach a version of the Halloway secret by different doors. |
